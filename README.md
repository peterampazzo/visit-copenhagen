Things to know 🧯

Public transport:

The Metro runs 24/7.

Paper tickets do not need to be validated.

An alternative is the Copenhagen Card, which includes public transport and museum admission: copenhagencard.com 

Plastic/cans: Don't throw them away; you can return them to the supermarket for a refund.

Getting around by bike:

Lights are mandatory after sunset.

Always raise your arm before stopping.

Tourists do rent bikes, please follow the rules (https://politi.dk/-/media/mediefiler/kbh/billeder-diverse/how-to-cycle-in-denmark-(1).pdf) 



Places & Things 🏰

Nyhavn

Christiania Pusher Street has been closed, but it's a nice area to walk around.

Søerne (The Lakes)

Nordhavn One of the new neighborhoods, on an artificial peninsula, with the waterbus terminal.

Nørrebro A vibrant, historic neighborhood known for being one of Copenhagen's most multi-ethnic and culturally diverse areas.

Amager Strand: A beach park.

Tivoli An amusement park in the city center, you can also just get in for a walk and there’re restaurants in it.

Superkilen A park in the Nørrebro district, featured in several commercials.

CopenHill / Amager Bakke: An incinerator with a ski slope on top. You can go up for free with the elevator, but it's a bit out of the way [nothing fancy]

Kongens Have (The King's Garden) The Royal Gardens, with a rose garden.

Amalienborg The Royal Palace.

Kastellet The Citadel, an army fortress with a beautiful park and cherry trees.

Magstræde A historic street.

There are several castles north of Copenhagen, reachable by train.

Go to the Parliament, take the elevator and see the view from the top of the tower, no need to book and it’s for free.

Take the train and visit Malmö (Sweden) crossing the Øresundsbridge

With the public transport ticket you can also get the waterbus which across the main channel (locals call it “Copenhagen Harbour”), check Citymapper for the times, it’s usually one every half an hour.



Food 😋

Broens Gadekøkken The Bridge Street Kitchen, just on the other side of Nyhavn [outdoor]



Danish cuisine isn't anything special, but smørrebrød (an open-faced sandwich) is popular with tourists. 

You can find a good version at Torvehallerne market (https://share.google/mTnctOOOQcl2mNZXC), and the ones at Aamanns 1921 are also very good (https://share.google/SBy6GWdlYLLtZitEX), check the prices.



For a good, typical Danish restaurant, try Det Lille Apotek (https://share.google/uK69ZSpmLYuXoaC9h).
